DRYER OPTIMIZER SESSION DOCUMENTATION
=====================================

Last updated: 2026-08-13
Project: E:/Projects/lotus_power

PURPOSE
-------
Reduced-order 2D Y-Z airflow topology optimizer for the build123d tray dryer.
The engineering objective is to distribute air more uniformly over exactly 20
trays while limiting baffle material and pressure demand. The output is a CAD/
CFD candidate, not a replacement for final 3D validation.

USER INPUTS
-----------
CAD source: test_files/sldw_cad.py
Fan curve:
  Q [m3/s]  = 0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00
  dP [Pa]   = 1800, 1650, 1300, 700, 0
Fan: 315 mm plug fan; the reduced model uses 0.315 m out-of-plane width.
Required CAD constraints: 20 trays; total height 1.6-1.7 m.
Derived dimensions: tray stack 1.280 m; mechanical room 0.350 m; total 1.630 m;
side-section airflow depth 0.702 m.

CURRENT PHYSICS
---------------
The optimizer now spans the FULL 1.630 m height so the fan actuator is inside
its mechanical room. Horizontal grid coordinate is airflow depth/CAD Y; vertical
coordinate is CAD Z from cabinet bottom.

PhysicsConfig defaults:
  air density = 1.20 kg/m3
  molecular viscosity = 1.8e-5 Pa s
  eddy viscosity closure = 2.0e-4 Pa s
  effective viscosity = 2.18e-4 Pa s
  alpha_max = 1e5
  Brinkman q = 0.05
  fan diameter = 0.315 m
  fan thickness = 0.120 m
  fan source direction = -1.0 (see direction note below)
  Newton max iterations = 120
  Newton relaxation = 0.30, with residual backtracking and warm starts

The equations are steady incompressible Navier-Stokes with Brinkman friction:
  rho*(u.grad)u = -grad(p) + mu_eff*laplacian(u) - alpha*u + f_fan
  div(u) = 0

The solver uses a staggered MAC grid, first-order upwind convection, sparse
SciPy matrices, and damped Newton iterations. The internal fan source is
calculated from the fan curve:
  Q_fan = b * sum(u_fan_face * dy)
  f_fan = dP(Q_fan) / fan_thickness
where b=0.315 m. The curve is piecewise linear and the exact segment slope
(dP/dQ) is included in the final residual Jacobian.

The model is a SEALED box: top/bottom and all side faces are no-slip walls and
the internal fan is the only momentum source. There is no prescribed velocity
inlet and no external opening; the fan's pressure rise recirculates the air
through the tray stack (supply plenum -> trays -> return plenum -> fan). The
global pressure null space is anchored at p = 0 gauge in the four wall corners
and in a fluid cell at the center of the mechanical room
(row = ny - ny//8, col = nx//2), which is guaranteed to sit above the tray
stack, outside the fan actuator, and off the walls. The retained
left_opening_flow/right_opening_flow diagnostics are identically zero in the
sealed configuration.

Fan direction note: fan_source_direction = -1.0 in PhysicsConfig. It is the
sign factor in Q_fan = dir * b * dy * sum(u_fan_face). With -1.0, positive
Q_fan means air flowing in the correct physical direction
(supply -> trays -> return); the forward solution respects this orientation.

DESIGN VARIABLE SEMANTICS
-------------------------
Saved array name: density. It is SOLID fraction rho_s = 1-gamma.
  density=0 -> fluid gamma=1
  density=1 -> solid baffle gamma=0

Brinkman interpolation:
  alpha = alpha_min + (alpha_max-alpha_min)*q*rho_s/(q+1-rho_s)
with exact derivative used by the adjoint. Geometry-locked solids are forced
to alpha_max and cannot be design cells.

OBJECTIVE
---------
For each of 20 tray sample lines, average horizontal velocity v_i is computed.
The target is derived from the SIGNED fan flow (the actual mass throughput of
the internal fan), split equally among 20 tray lines and converted to a
velocity using the fan's out-of-plane width and the tray span:
  v_target = Q_fan/(20*b*span),  span = max(tray_depth, dx)
  J_uniformity = mean((v_i-v_target)^2)/velocity_reference^2
The signed fan flow (not its absolute value) is used so that
Q_fan and dQ_fan/du_face = dir*b*dy are continuous through Q = 0; abs() would
create a gradient discontinuity at the shutoff operating point and corrupt the
adjoint at startup and during Newton warm-starts whenever the solver passes
through Q = 0.

Additional objective terms:
  material_cost_weight * solid_fraction
  volume_constraint_weight * quadratic penalty outside 5%-15% solid fraction
  pressure_constraint_weight * quadratic penalty above 300 Pa fan pressure

This prevents a fake solution consisting of a nearly solid wall with tiny
pinholes. The pressure constraint is a penalty in the current V1; a future
augmented-Lagrangian or explicit constrained update can make it harder.

ADJOINT
-------
At each converged nonlinear state, FlowState.matrix is the analytic Jacobian
of the full discrete residual. It includes:
  - Brinkman resistance derivatives;
  - upwind convection derivatives with respect to both transported and
    advecting velocities;
  - dense low-rank fan dP/dQ coupling;
  - continuity and pressure boundary rows.

The adjoint solves J.T lambda = dJ/dstate and then evaluates the density
sensitivity. dJ/dstate is exact and combines three coupled pieces:
  1. the tray-uniformity SSE with the fan-flow-dependent target, via the chain
     rule dJ/du_face = dJ/dv_target * dv_target/dQ * dQ/du_face with
     dQ/du_face = fan_source_direction * b * dy;
  2. the pressure-cap penalty, dJ_p/dQ = w * 2*excess/dP_ref^2 * dP/dQ (the
     exact piecewise-linear fan-curve slope), combined with the same dQ/du_face;
  3. the explicit material/volume terms (returned separately).
fan_source_direction is owned by PhysicsConfig and is threaded through
compute_adjoint -> objective_state_gradient. It is a REQUIRED keyword argument
of objective_state_gradient, so a non-default direction can no longer silently
produce a wrong-signed gradient (the earlier code accidentally defaulted it to
-1.0 regardless of configuration). A central finite-difference check on a 12x24
grid checks three design cells and passes with error below 2e-4, including the
Q=0 startup state; a regression test additionally verifies that flipping
fan_source_direction exactly flips the sign of the fan-face gradient entries.
The current direct check observed a maximum absolute error of about 1.7e-7
after the final target/physics corrections.

Key findings that matter for the production solver:
  1. The existing architecture is the right family: discrete adjoint on the
     converged Jacobian (topflow), MAC grid (Stokes_TO), Borrvall-Petersson
     rational law (topflow/Stokes_TO), chain-rule-transpose filter.
  2. Reference priority corrected by the source evidence: topflow is the
     primary reference for most stages (adjoint, OC update, interpolation,
     0.5-contour -> DXF export); Stokes_TO first for the finite-difference
     gradient checker and the MMA update path; cashocs for the pressure-drop
     objective (pipe-bend). This supersedes the STO > TF > CA ordering
     suggested when the repos were acquired.
  3. GAP classification of the current code: 5 COMPLETE, 3 MISSING (OC update,
     DXF export, Brinkman-penalty continuation on q), 4 PARTIAL, 0 INCORRECT,
     2 NEEDS_VERIFICATION.
  4. Three most serious correctness risks: (a) the target-velocity degeneracy
     below; (b) the adjoint finite-difference test covers only a reduced
     objective (pressure/volume/material weights zeroed) at a single epsilon;
     (c) no analytic-flow or mass-conservation tests for the forward solver.
  5. Three biggest performance bottlenecks: (a) per-cell Python assembly loops;
     (b) no LU factorization reuse between the primal and adjoint solves (the
     adjoint re-factorizes A^T); (c) Newton backtracking re-assembles the full
     matrix per trial. Measured: 16,288 DOF at 48x112; forward solve ~188 s,
     24x56 ~24 s, 16x32 a few seconds. A 30-iteration 48x112 run is ~1.5-2 h
     and does not yet meet the seconds-to-minutes target.
  Nothing in the production solver was changed during these studies.

  .venv/Scripts/python.exe -m dryer_optimizer.main --iterations 30 --nx 48 --ny 112

The default-grid Newton solve is materially more expensive than the old linear
Stokes model because each design iteration requires repeated sparse nonlinear
factorizations. Warm-start continuation is used between topology iterations.
Use 24x56 to iterate on topology and 48x112 for a final check.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS / DO NOT CLAIM
--------------------------------
- 2D slice cannot model 315 mm plug-fan swirl, blade effects, or discharge
  profile; calibrate against 3D CFD.
- Effective eddy viscosity is a closure, not a turbulence model.
- The 2D model is a sealed box: there is no external make-up air, and the
  retained opening-flow diagnostics are identically zero by construction.
- On grids coarser than roughly 16x32 the fan is under-resolved inside the
  350 mm mechanical room and the Newton solve can converge to a Q=0 local
  recirculation instead of through-tray circulation; use 16x32 or finer.
- Tray perforations are represented by reduced resistance; CAD tray solids need
  porous/perforated treatment in Fluent/FloEFD.
- Density filtering/projection and volume/pressure penalties are not a global
  manufacturing-cost optimum.
- Target-velocity degeneracy (flagged in research/IMPLEMENTATION_GAP_ANALYSIS.md):
  the uniformity target v_t = Q_fan/(20*b*span) scales with the fan flow the
  optimizer itself controls, so the optimizer is structurally rewarded for
  reducing Q (uniformity trivially improves and the pressure cap loosens).
  Must be resolved (fixed target, CV-only objective, or minimum-velocity term)
  before optimized topologies are trusted.
- No heat, humidity, evaporation, species, transient, 3D, SolidWorks, or Fluent
  automation has been added.
